Section 232 · US Midwest aluminium premium · research summary

Has the Midwest premium priced in the Section 232 tariffs?

The physical premium did, in full. The futures curve did not. Import parity explains the premium at the statutory 25% and 50% rates, while the forward curve kept pricing a relief that never came. The tariff also raised the LME hedge ratio of US-delivered metal above 1 and pushed Canadian metal toward Europe.

1

Price: the premium passed the tariff through in full

Import parity is what it costs to land a Canadian tonne in the Midwest: LME + its European alternative premium (Rotterdam duty-paid, netted back) + freight + the tariff on that value. If the premium sits at parity, the tariff is fully passed through.

2

Futures: the curve priced a relief that never came

At each month-end, the M03 contract is the market's forecast of the premium two months out. It is tested against two benchmarks, no change and Canada parity at the rate in force, with Newey–West errors. Forecasts whose horizon spans a tariff step are left out of the statistics.

3

Risk: a 1:1 LME hedge is too small under an ad valorem tariff

A tariff charged on value makes the parity premium move with LME: ∂MWP/∂LME = t. A tonne of US-delivered metal then moves like 1 + t tonnes of LME, so at 50% theory says 1.5.

4

Flows: Canadian metal went to Europe, and the US bought semis and scrap

Read these results with the caveats

  • Freight is an assumption. No free series exists, so every parity result carries a low–high band. Before 2025 the Canada route sat 80–180 $/t above parity at a 0% tariff, so small gaps are not arbitrage.
  • Hormuz is a separate shock. The Strait closed on 2 Mar 2026 and drove the jump in Rotterdam premiums and the fall in Gulf imports. Tariff attribution uses months up to Feb 2026.
  • The LME MW futures curve is stale. Back months are unchanged on ~80% of days, and the Aug 2026 move showed up two days late and at half the CME size. Stale quotes are flagged in every test.
  • Small samples. The 50% forecast test has 7 overlapping forecasts from one regime. The trading rule is in-sample and before costs.
  • Rotterdam duty-unpaid after Oct 2025 is reconstructed from duty-paid at an effective EU duty of 2.7% (backtest error ±13 $/t). The Canada route uses duty-paid, which is observed.
  • Aug 2026 is not fully fixed. The last premium is M01 on 28 Aug. The CME move is from press reports, not settlement data.
