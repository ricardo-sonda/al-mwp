How the physical price changed after the 50% tariff (so absolute differences in real aluminium price in the MW before and after tariffs) and compare it to the "parity price", i.e. the modelled price of "expected price after tariffs" = LME + MW premium = LME + European duty unpaid premium + tariff cost + freight. The differece of these two quantities (theoretical minus physical) will tell us the extent to which the tariffs are priced in the physical, i.e. whether the tariffs costs get fully passed to consumers (in case of no difference). In the 100% pass-through case, a decrease in imported aluminium would only be due to supply-demand / willingess-to-pay dynamics. However, the likely scenario is less than 100% passthrough, and that gap is what is interesting, because it tells us the other ways the US is coping with tariffs, such as using up US inventories or that the markets are anticipating a tariff reversal.
Compare the landed cost (LME + premium + tariff + freight) then compare to the MW physical price. The difference tells you the import margin, whether it's profitable to bring in a ton from say, Canada, versus Erurope.
